# H-Bridge

## What is an H-Bridge?

- Basic definition - electronic circuit that allows you to control the speed and direction of a DC motor
- When current flows through the motor, this generates a magnetic field (current-carrying wires inside the motor cause the creation of this field) causing the rotor to spin
- The greater the current, the greater the speed of the motor
- Reversing current direction reverses direction of rotation

## How Does The H-Bridge Work?

- H-Bridge driver lets you reverse the rotation of the motor through the use of switches
  - Very basic diagram (looks like an H, thus H-bridge)
  - The way the switches are toggled changes the direction of the current and the rotational direction of the motor. 1 and 4 allow the motor to rotate clockwise while 2 and 3 allow it to rotate counterclockwise
  - Transistors are used instead of switches so that you do not have to manually press the buttons to switch the direction of the motor
    - Transistor - a semiconductor device that controls the flow of electrical signals (current)
    - When a small electrical current is applied to the transistor base, it allows a larger current to flow between the collector and the emitter. This is like a valve: a little pressure on the base controls a much bigger flow of electricity.
    - A transistor acts like a switch, but the condition for opening and closing is if there is a current at the base (middle pin) of the MOSFET transistor
  - N-channel MOSFETs are used for switches 2 and 4
    - When voltage is applied (from an MCU) to this MOSFET it starts conducting, allowing current to flow into the motor (acting like a closed switch)
    - When no voltage is applied, it stops conducting (acting like an open switch)
  - P-channel MOSFETs are used for switches 1 and 3
    - When voltage is applied (from an MCU) to this MOSFET it stops conducting (acting like an open switch)
    - When no voltage is applied it starts conducting, allowing current to flow through the motor
  - Why are N-channels not exclusively used
    - For the N-channel MOSFET, the gate voltage has to be at least 5V higher than the voltage at the MOSFET pins for it to conduct properly and since the gates 1 and 3 are already connected to a 5V supply, that would be the gate voltage would have to be at least 10V which is more difficult to achieve with an MCU
    - P-channel MOSFETs require the gate voltage to be at least 5V lower than the voltage at the pins to conduct properly, so they are used at the top as generating a 5V signal is much easier than 10V as another circuit would need to be used for a 10V signal (since the MCU can only output 5V)

## Building the Circuit

- To use the H-bridge with an Arduino (or any MCU) resistors need to in series with the MOSFET gates to limit the current

### Use of Flyback Diodes

- When using components that generate magnetic fields or any component sensitive to voltage spikes, it is important to use protection against inductive spikes generated by the rotor.
- Inductive spike - happens when current suddenly stops flowing through the winding of the motor, thus causing the magnetic field to collapse and a spike in voltage. This can fry the MOSFETs
- When Q1 and Q4 are switched off, the motor's inductance will maintain the current in the same direction it was flowing in (in this instance from left to right)
  - D2 becomes forward biased as the voltage at its cathode drops below battery voltage and D3 becomes forward biased as the voltage at its anode rises above ground
  - Forward biased - the voltage across the diode allows for current to flow in its natural state
  - The current will follow the outlined path, allowing for the inductive energy to dissipate safely

## H-Bridge and Microcontrollers

- Connect the microcontroller to the gates of all 4 transistors

### Speed

- Determined by the average voltage of the motor; high voltage results in higher speed
- To adjust the voltage, pulse width modulation which means the voltage turns on and off really fast to create a lower average voltage
- The duty cycle determines for what percentage of one period is the voltage on

## ICs and Modules

- ICs are used because they are cheaper, more efficient, and may have more features that better protect the motor and the H-bridge itself

## H-Bridge PCB

- This PCB layout was made in Fusion
- When designing a PCB layout, the most important thing is keeping the components as close together as possible to reduce the amount of noise as longer transmission lines result in a greater likelihood of stronger noise to interfere with the signal that we are trying to output and receive as well. Therefore, this PCB has all the components as close together as possible.
